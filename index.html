Laptop → Phone experiments

Five ways to get text and files from a Mac to an Android phone. Each one is its own page, so we can compare them side by side.

Start here: open the scanner on your phone

Point your phone camera at this code to open the scanner. In Chrome, use “Add to Home screen” to install it as an app.

1 · One QR code for text

Type or paste on the Mac and a QR code appears. “Plain” codes open in any camera app; “Compact” codes squeeze in about twice as much text and need our scanner.

2 · Animated QR (and colour QR)

Send images and small files as a looping QR animation with fountain codes. Turn on colour mode to pack 3 codes into each frame (approach 4).

3 · Direct peer-to-peer

WebRTC with no signalling server: the phone scans the Mac, the Mac’s webcam scans the phone, then files flow directly at Wi-Fi speed.

5 · AirDrop via the share sheet

The text and animated pages have a “Share…” button that opens the Mac share menu. AirDrop to Android works only on Pixel 10 phones, with AirDrop set to “Everyone for 10 minutes”.

Existing tools to compare against

  • PairDrop: web app, QR pairing, WebRTC peer-to-peer.
  • LocalSend: native app, same Wi-Fi only, no server.
  • cimbar: colour barcode at about 100 KB/s; the receiver is the CameraFileCopy Android app.
